Hiring someone to build your website is a decision you will live with for years. A good website brings enquiries, sales and trust while you sleep. A bad one costs you money twice — once to build it, and again to rebuild it.

This guide is for startups, small businesses and e-commerce brands who need a website, an app or custom software — and want to hire the right team the first time. It covers what to ask before you hire, freelancer vs agency, cost, timeline, ownership, and the red flags that should make you walk away.

Start with your goal, not with a design

Before you talk to any agency, write down one sentence: what should this website do for your business?

  • "Bring me 10 quote requests a month from local customers."
  • "Sell my products online without me taking orders on WhatsApp."
  • "Make my clinic look trustworthy so patients book an appointment."
  • "Replace our spreadsheets with one simple system our team can use."

A good agency will start with that sentence and plan the site around it. A bad agency will start with colours and fonts. If the first meeting is about design before it is about your goal, that is a warning sign.

Your goal also decides what you need: a brochure site, an online store, a booking system, a mobile app, or custom software. A web design agency in Dera Ghazi Khan should be able to explain which one fits — and when a simpler option would do.

Freelancer vs agency — which is better?

There is no universal right answer. The right choice depends on your project.

A freelancer is a good fit for a clearly defined, one-time project, if you can give clear instructions and review work quickly. You talk directly to the person doing the work, and the cost is usually lower. A skilled freelancer can be perfect for a simple website with a clear scope and a tight budget.

A small agency is a good fit when you need design, copy, development and support to work together, and you want one team accountable for the result. An agency suits you better when the project needs several skills at once, or when you expect the website to grow. You get a team, a structured process, and someone to call after launch. It usually costs more.

A large agency is a good fit only for large projects with many stakeholders. For most small businesses, a large agency means higher prices and an account manager instead of the person building your site.

The type of provider matters less than the answers to the ten questions below — and what the contract says about ownership. A freelancer with a portfolio, a contract and references is accountable. An agency with a portfolio, a contract and references is accountable. If either one cannot show you these things, do not hire them.

The 10 questions to ask before you hire

Ask these in your first call, and write the answers down. A good agency will answer clearly. A bad one will change the subject.

1. Can you show me live websites you have built?

Ask for three to five live websites, not screenshots. Open them yourself. Click around. Do they load quickly? Do they work on your phone? Can you find the phone number and contact form without hunting? Ask what the agency actually did on each project, and whether you can speak to one of those clients.

A portfolio is a set of screenshots chosen by the person selling to you. Live websites you can visit and test are the real proof.

2. Who will actually do the work?

Will the person you are speaking with design the website? Will someone else develop it? Is SEO handled internally? Is any part outsourced? Either answer can be fine — but you have a right to know. With PrimeLoft, you work directly with the founders — the people you talk to are the people building your site.

3. What exactly is included in the price?

Ask for an itemised quote. Does it include design and development? Content writing? Domain and hosting? Basic SEO setup? WhatsApp integration and forms? How many revision rounds are included, and what costs extra? Also ask about recurring costs: domain renewal, hosting renewal, and maintenance.

4. How long will the project take?

Marketing sites, WordPress builds and Squarespace launches typically take 2 to 4 weeks. Full-scale SaaS platforms, custom software and native mobile apps generally take 6 to 12 weeks. Ask what the milestones are, and what happens if the timeline slips. Be wary of projects where you pay everything and see the result only at the end — ask for a prototype or progress before the second payment.

5. Who owns the code, domain and hosting?

This is the most important question in this guide. The right answer is: you do, 100%. Your domain, hosting, analytics and repository should be in accounts in your name from day one, with the agency added as a user. On milestone settlement, all source code, design systems, Figma artboards, databases and architectural assets should be transferred directly to your own Git repositories and cloud accounts. Zero vendor lock-in, ever.

If the answer is "we hold the domain for you" or "the code stays with us", walk away.

6. Is SEO included, or is that a separate cost?

Ask exactly what is included. A good build includes the basics: clean headings, proper page hierarchy, meta titles and descriptions, mobile-first layout, and Google Search Console. Ongoing SEO services — keyword work, technical audits, Core Web Vitals optimisation and link building — are usually a separate monthly service. Be careful of anyone who promises "page 1 in 30 days".

7. What is your development process?

A professional should be able to explain the job in plain stages: understanding your business, planning the pages, showing you a design before building, developing, testing, then launching. At PrimeLoft the four steps are: Define the direction, Design the experience, Build and test, Launch and support. If the agency cannot explain its process in plain language, it probably does not have one.

8. What happens after launch?

Who fixes bugs, how fast, and what a change costs? If there is a monthly plan, what does it cover? Post-launch support for bug fixes and performance tuning should be included in your project scope or offered as an ongoing maintenance arrangement covering security updates, infrastructure monitoring, backups and feature iterations.

9. How will I know it is working?

A good website is measured: enquiries, quote requests, sales. Ask what you will be able to see and how often. A monthly report that shows which keywords moved, which pages gained traffic, and what was done is worth more than a dashboard you never open.

10. What happens if you become unavailable?

If the agency disappears, can another person take over from what you have been given? This is where logins, a repository and a short handover note decide whether you are stuck. It is also why ownership matters so much — if you own everything, you can switch to anyone.

Red flags that should make you walk away

  • No real portfolio you can visit. Screenshots only, no live links, or "our clients asked us not to share" for every project.
  • A quote so cheap it cannot possibly include testing and support. Somebody is paying for the missing work — usually you, later.
  • Full payment demanded before any work starts. Milestone payments tied to delivered work are the professional standard.
  • No written agreement. A verbal promise or a chat message is hard to rely on later.
  • No clear answer on ownership. If the agency will not put "you own everything" in writing, do not proceed.
  • Their own website is slow, broken, or outdated. A web agency that cannot build a good site for itself will not build one for you. Open their site on your phone before the first call.
  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed sales. Nobody can guarantee Google rankings. Anyone who does is selling a promise they cannot keep.

Squarespace vs WordPress — which should the agency recommend?

A good agency will recommend the platform by the job, not by what it prefers to sell.

WordPress suits most business websites because it is flexible and easy to manage. WordPress development is the right choice when you want full control, custom features, or a site that will grow. WordPress powers over 43% of the entire web.

Squarespace suits a simple, beautiful site you can manage yourself. Squarespace development is the right choice for a brochure site, a portfolio, or a small online store that does not need custom software.

Ask the agency to explain its recommendation in one paragraph. If it cannot, it is selling a platform, not solving your problem.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a small business website cost?

Most small businesses spend between \$500 and \$15,000 for an initial build, plus hosting and maintenance. Freelancers typically quote $1,500–$8,000; agencies commonly quote $5,000–$25,000+ depending on pages, e-commerce and integrations. Read the full breakdown in how much does a small business website cost.

How long does it take to build a website?

Marketing sites, WordPress builds and Squarespace launches typically take 2 to 4 weeks. Full-scale SaaS platforms, custom software and native mobile apps generally take 6 to 12 weeks.

Is it better to hire a freelancer or an agency?

It depends on the project. A freelancer or small studio gives you direct contact and a lower price; a large agency has more people for complex projects. What matters is knowing who does the work, seeing live websites they built, and keeping everything in your name.

What should I ask before hiring a web agency?

Ask to see live websites, ask who will do the work, ask what is included in the price, ask how long it will take, and ask who owns the code, domain and hosting. The answers will tell you more than any portfolio PDF.

Who owns my website after it is built?

You should, 100% — domain, hosting, content, source code and design files, in accounts in your name. PrimeLoft transfers everything to your own Git repositories and cloud accounts on milestone settlement. Zero vendor lock-in, ever.

The bottom line

Choosing a web design agency is not about finding the cheapest quote or the biggest team. It is about finding the team whose live work you can open on your phone, whose process you understand, whose quote is written down, and whose contract says you own everything.

Ask the ten questions. Visit the live sites. Talk to one past client. And do not pay anything until the scope, the timeline and the ownership are in writing.
